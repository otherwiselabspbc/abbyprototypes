## Overview

Reading development follows 5 phases. Each phase represents a qualitatively distinct approach to reading. Development is **linear within each domain** but **non-linear across domains** — a child may progress faster in one area than another.

The 4 main domains are:
1. **Print Concepts** — understanding that text carries meaning
2. **Phonological Awareness** — hearing and manipulating sounds in words
3. **Orthographic Knowledge** — knowing letters, their names, and visual patterns
4. **Alphabetic Principle / Decoding** — connecting letters to sounds to read words

---

## Phase 1: Pre-Alphabetic

Children read using non-letter visual cues (icons, logos, word shapes).
*Example: sees stop sign and uses red color or shape to read as "stop"*

**Existing:** Oral language, tacit phonological/syntactic knowledge, semantic knowledge
**Missing:** Print concepts, explicit phonological awareness, orthographic knowledge

### Targeted Skills

**Print Concepts**
- Print/Text Identification
  - Level 1: Distinguishes letters/symbols from other visuals
  - Level 2: Distinguishes orthographic symbols from all other symbols
- Text Awareness
  - Level 1: Aware of words as unique collections of symbols
  - Level 2a: Aware of sentences as unique collections of words
  - Level 2b: Aware of directionality of orthography

**Phonological Awareness**
- Rhyme Detection/Generation
  - Level 1: Recognizes rhyming words share similar endings
  - Level 2: Identifies rhyming vs. non-rhyming words
  - Level 3: Generates rhyming word(s)
- Syllable Blending/Segmenting
  - Level 1: Blend compound words
  - Level 2: Blend syllables
  - Level 3: Clap syllables
- Phoneme Blending
  - Level 1: Blend CV/VC words
  - Level 2: Blend CVC words

**Orthographic Knowledge**
- Letter Identification (Letter Names)
  - Level 1: Knows name of a letter (usually first letter) in own name
  - Level 2: Knows names of at least 5 letters (usually letters in own name)
  - Level 3: Knows names of at least 15 letters
- Letter Formation
  - Level 1: Can write one letter when visual stimulus is present
  - Level 2: Can write letters in name
  - Level 3: Can write over half of letters

**Alphabetic Principle**
- Letter-Sound Correspondences
  - Level 1: Knows name of a letter and its main associated sound (e.g., "T" says /t/)
  - Level 2: Knows name and main associated sound of at least two letters
  - Level 3: Knows name and main associated sound of at least five letters

## Phase 2: Partial Alphabetic

Children have incomplete knowledge of the alphabetic principle but DO use their limited knowledge when attempting to read.
*Example: sees first letter of name in word and uses that letter's sound to guess word*

**Existing:** Oral language, print concepts, letter ID, letter formation
**Missing:** Complete phonological awareness, complete orthographic knowledge, complete alphabetic principle, decoding

### Targeted Skills

**Phonological Awareness**
- Phoneme Blending
  - Grade K / Level 1: CV/VC → CVC
  - Grade 1 / Level 2: CVC → CCVC/CVCC
  - Grade 1 / Level 3: CVC → CCVCC
  - Grade 2 / Level 4: CCVC/CVCC → CCVCC
  - Grade 2 / Level 5: CCVCC → CCCVCC/CCVCCC
- Phoneme Segmenting
  - Grade K / Level 1: CV/VC
  - Grade K / Level 2: CVC
  - Grade 1 / Level 3: CVC → CCVC/CVCC
  - Grade 2 / Level 4: CVC → CCVCC

**Orthographic Knowledge**
- Fluent Letter Identification (Letter Naming Fluency)
  - Grade K / Level 1: 37 letters/minute
  - Grade K / Level 2: 42 letters/minute
  - Grade 1 / Level 3: 57 letters/minute
  - Grade 1 / Level 4: 59 letters/minute
  - Grade 2 / Level 5: 61 letters/minute
- Fast Letter Formation (Handwriting)
  - Grade K / Level 1: 15 letters upper/lower case
  - Grade K / Level 2: 20 letters upper/lower case
  - Grade 1 / Level 3: 26 letters upper/lower case

**Alphabetic Principle**
- Letter-Sound Correspondences
  - Grade K / Level 1: 10 letters (a, i, o + common consonants)
  - Grade K / Level 2: 20 letters (short vowels + most consonants)
  - Grade 1 / Level 3: All consonants, short vowels
  - Grade 1 / Level 4: All consonants, short and long vowels (VCe)
  - Grade 1 / Level 5: Vowel and consonant digraphs
  - Grade 1 / Level 6: Soft c/g

**Decoding**
- Grade K / Level 1: CV/VC
- Grade K / Level 2: CVC
- Grade 1 / Level 3: CVC → CCVC/CVCC
- Grade 2 / Level 4: CVC → CCVCC

---

## Phase 3: Full Alphabetic

Children have complete knowledge of the alphabetic principle and use it to decode words. Repeated decoding strengthens orthographic mapping → automatic recognition.
*Example: decodes "rat" multiple times, strengthening orthographic representation so it can be read fluently without sounding out*

**Existing:** All Phase 1-2 skills, consonants/short vowels/long vowels/digraphs, decoding CVC → CCVCC
**Missing:** Complex phonological awareness, complex orthographic knowledge, decoding longer words

### Targeted Skills

**Phonological Awareness**
- Syllable Blending
  - Grade 2 / Level 1: 2-3 syllables
  - Grade 2 / Level 2: 3-5 syllables
- Syllable Segmenting
  - Grade 2 / Level 1: 2-3 syllables
  - Grade 2 / Level 2: 3-5 syllables

**Orthographic Knowledge**
- Complex Orthographic Patterns
  - Grade 2 / Level 1: Consonant patterns (e.g., dge, tch, doubling)
  - Grade 2 / Level 2: R-controlled vowels
  - Grade 2 / Level 3: Diphthongs
  - Grade 2 / Level 4: Simple pre-/suffixes
  - Grade 3 / Level 5: Common pre-/suffixes

**Decoding**
- Grade 2 / Level 1: CVC → CCVCC
- Grade 2 / Level 2: 2-syllable words
- Grade 2 / Level 3: 3-syllable words
- Grade 2 / Level 4: 4+ syllable words

---

## Phase 4: Consolidated Alphabetic

Children have advanced knowledge of orthography including general patterns and larger orthographic units.
*Example: recognize morpheme syllables like -ing or pre- to quickly decode "tripping" and "prevent"*

**Note:** Children at this phase would NOT benefit from the foundational reading games designed for dyslexia. Games for consolidated readers should focus on keeping readers engaged and strengthening higher-order cognitive skills for reading.

---

## Phase 5: Automatic

Children rapidly and effortlessly read words and can devote full cognitive resources to reading comprehension.

**Note:** Same as Phase 4 — these children don't need foundational reading skill games. Focus on engagement and higher-order comprehension.

## Key Design Implications

### Non-linear progression across domains
A child may be at Level 3 in Letter Identification but Level 1 in Phoneme Blending. Games must account for this — don't assume all Phase 1 skills are mastered before moving to Phase 2 skills.

### Our games target Phases 1-3
Phases 4-5 don't benefit from foundational reading games. Our game library should cover:
- **Phase 1:** Print concepts, rhyme, syllables, early phoneme blending, letter ID, letter formation, early letter-sound correspondences
- **Phase 2:** Phoneme blending/segmenting (increasingly complex), letter fluency, handwriting speed, expanding letter-sound knowledge, early decoding
- **Phase 3:** Syllable-level work, complex orthographic patterns, multi-syllable decoding

## How to Use This Skill

When designing a new game:
1. **Identify the phase** — which phase is this game for?
2. **Identify the domain** — which of the 4 domains?
3. **Identify the specific skill and level** — use the progression above
4. **Check prerequisites** — does the player need skills from earlier levels?
5. **Check what's already covered** — use the mapping table above to avoid duplicates
6. **Prioritize gaps** — build games for missing skills before adding more games for covered skills
